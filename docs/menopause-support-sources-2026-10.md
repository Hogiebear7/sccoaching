# Menopause support: sources (October 2026)

Sources behind slice H. The advice text lives in `lib/menopause.ts`, with an
evidence label on each point. The bar: guideline bodies, systematic reviews
and randomised trials. Popular claims without trials behind them stay out
(cycle syncing, fasted training for women, supplements sold for hot
flushes), and the app never advises on HRT or other treatment: that's
always "talk to your GP".

## Stages

- Perimenopause: periods still come but are changing; symptoms may start.
  Cycle tracking continues, with phase estimates marked low confidence.
- Post-menopause: 12 months or more since the last period (NICE NG23).
  Phase tracking and the luteal +120 kcal shift are switched off.
- The member chooses the stage; the app doesn't diagnose it.

## Training

- Strength training: progressive resistance work for the major muscle
  groups, building up to heavy loads (the most you can lift 8–12 times),
  plus impact exercise, to keep bones strong; balance work to reduce falls
  (Strong, steady and straight: UK consensus statement on physical activity
  and exercise for osteoporosis, BJSM 2022;56:837–846, endorsed by the Royal
  Osteoporosis Society). Evidence label: strong.
- Heavy, supervised strength and impact training raised spine bone density
  in post-menopausal women with low bone mass, without more fractures
  (LIFTMOR, Watson et al., JBMR 2018;33:211–220; a later analysis in
  Osteoporosis International). Small and closely supervised, so the app
  says "with a coach or physio" for anyone with low bone density. Evidence
  label: good, from one trial.
- NICE NG23 (recommendation 1.2.5, 2015, amended 2024) asks clinicians to
  explain why muscle mass and strength matter and how activity maintains
  them, but sets no training doses.
- 150 minutes of moderate activity a week, and strength work on 2 or more
  days (WHO physical activity guidelines). Evidence label: strong.
- Hot flushes: exercise has not been shown to reduce them (Cochrane review
  CD006108, Daley et al. 2014, low-quality evidence). The app does not claim
  it does; NICE 2024 points to CBT and medical treatment through the GP.

## Nutrition

- Protein: at least 1.0–1.2 g per kg for healthy older adults, 1.2 g per kg
  or more when active (PROT-AGE, Bauer et al. 2013; ESPEN). The app already
  sets 1.6–2.0 g per kg for everyone, so there's no change, only an
  explanation.
- Calcium: three portions of calcium-rich food a day (milk, yoghurt,
  cheese); a 500 mg supplement for those eating less than one dairy portion
  a day (FSAI scientific recommendations for older adults).
- Vitamin D: 15 µg a day from 65 (Department of Health, 2021, from the FSAI
  report). Under 65, ask the GP or pharmacist; many adults in Ireland run low.
- No blanket calorie cut: the calorie engine's burn already follows age,
  weight and the member's own logs.
