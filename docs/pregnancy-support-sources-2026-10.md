# Pregnancy support: sources (October 2026)

Sources behind the pregnancy, birth and breastfeeding features (slices A–G).
The advice text lives in `lib/pregnancy.ts`; the owner is having it checked by
a specialist as the pregnancy progresses, so changes go there.

## Calories

| Stage | Daily extra | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Trimester 1 (weeks 0–13) | none | HSE healthy eating in pregnancy |
| Trimester 2 (weeks 14–27) | about +250 kcal | HSE |
| Trimester 3 (weeks 28+) | up to +500 kcal | HSE |
| Breastfeeding | about +500 kcal | EFSA dietary reference values for energy (2013) |

No weight-loss deficit while pregnant or in the 12 weeks after the birth.
The HSE figures replace the US Institute of Medicine figures (+340/+450) the
app used before.

## Supplements and foods

- Folic acid 400 µg a day until week 12 (HSE national clinical guideline,
  Nutrition in Pregnancy). Higher doses for some women; that's the GP's call.
- Vitamin D 10 µg a day through pregnancy, and continued while breastfeeding
  (HSE guideline; HSE breastfeeding "what to eat").
- Iodine is hard to get from food in pregnancy and breastfeeding; pregnancy
  multivitamins usually carry 100–200 µg (HSE).
- Breastfeeding needs no special diet: limit some seafood, avoid alcohol,
  cut back on coffee (HSE breastfeeding "what to eat").

## Training

- At least 150 minutes of moderate activity a week over at least 3 days, aerobic
  and resistance, for women without contraindications (SOGC/CSEP 2019 Canadian
  guideline, Mottola et al., BJSM 2018;52:1339–1346).
- Already-active women can continue vigorous training; avoid contact sports and
  high fall risk; stop and seek care for bleeding, regular painful
  contractions, fluid leakage, breathlessness before exercise, dizziness,
  headache, chest pain, calf pain or swelling (ACOG Committee Opinion 804, 2020).
- Elite and highly trained athletes: IOC expert group, Exercise and pregnancy in
  recreational and elite athletes, parts 1–5 (BJSM 2016–2018). The evidence for
  most recommendations is rated very low to moderate.
- Return to running after the birth: not before about 3 months, after passing
  load and impact tests; a reasoning aid, not a fixed protocol (Goom, Donnelly
  & Brockwell, Returning to running postnatal, 2019).
